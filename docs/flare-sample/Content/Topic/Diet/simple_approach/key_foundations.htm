Practical Guidelines for Everyday Nutrition

Balance Your Plate

A healthy meal usually includes:

  • ½ vegetables & fruits: colorful produce provides fiber, vitamins, and antioxidants.

  • ¼ whole grains: brown rice, oats, quinoa, or whole wheat provide steady energy.

  • ¼ protein: beans, lentils, tofu, fish, eggs, or lean meats help repair and build muscle.

  • Healthy fats: nuts, seeds, avocado, or olive oil to support brain and heart health.

This approach (similar to Harvard’s Healthy Eating Plate) is simple to remember and adaptable to different cuisines.

Stay Hydrated

Water is essential for digestion, circulation, and mental focus. Aim for 7–9 cups daily. Herbal teas, lemon water, and coconut water are great alternatives to sugary drinks.

Eat Regularly and Mindfully

Skipping meals often leads to overeating later. Three balanced meals with one or two healthy snacks keep blood sugar steady. Just like in the yogic approach, avoid eating in front of screens or rushing through meals.

Limit Processed Foods

Packaged, fried, or sugary foods are high in salt, unhealthy fats, and preservatives. Instead of aiming for perfection, try the 80/20 rule: 80% wholesome, natural foods, 20% room for flexibility and enjoyment.

Practical Healthy Choices

  • Swap soda for sparkling water or infused water.

  • Choose baked or steamed dishes over fried ones.

  • Carry fruit, nuts, or roasted chickpeas as quick snacks instead of chips or sweets.

  • Plan meals ahead to avoid impulsive, less healthy choices.
