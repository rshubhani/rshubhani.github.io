Why Food Matters in Yoga

Yoga is more than just asanas and breathing exercises, it is a complete way of living. PatanjaliPatanjali was an ancient Indian sage and philosopher best known for compiling the Yoga Sutras, a foundational text for yoga, and for his work as a Sanskrit grammarian and commentator on texts related to Ayurveda (traditional medicine). The Yoga Sutras, a collection of aphorisms outlining the path to spiritual liberation, are considered the most authoritative text on classical yoga, making Patanjali the widely recognized "father of yoga"., in the Yoga Sutras, emphasized purity (sauchaSaucha is the Sanskrit word for purity or cleanliness, and it is the first of the Niyamas (personal observances) in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras. It involves the practice of both internal and external purity, including keeping your body and surroundings clean, maintaining a healthy diet, practicing self-care, and cultivating mental clarity.), discipline (tapasTapas refers to self-discipline, inner fire, and the practice of burning away negative habits and impurities to achieve spiritual and personal growth), and moderation (mitaharaMitahara refers to mindful, moderate, and pure eating in the yogic tradition, focusing on the quality and quantity of food to achieve physical and mental harmony.) as essential elements of a yogic lifestyle. This means that what we eat is not separate from our practice, but deeply connected to it.

Food is energy, and the quality of that energy shapes not only the body but also the mind. A calm, steady mind and a healthy body are easier to cultivate when the diet supports clarity and balance. Just as yoga trains the body to be flexible and the mind to be steady, a mindful diet trains the digestive system to function smoothly and the nervous system to remain calm.
